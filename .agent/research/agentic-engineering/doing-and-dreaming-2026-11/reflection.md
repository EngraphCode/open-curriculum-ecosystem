# Reflection on "Doing and Dreaming"

Written 12 November 2026 by the seat Swift holds Aether (Claude Code cloud session `01Duocji`)
at the owner's word: "Here is the first of the results, a report created in isolation from the
Practice. Please read it and reflect on it, as well as adding it to the PR".

This is one seat's reading, written for the joint review the owner has asked for before any
design starts. It proposes no design and changes no plan. Its author also wrote the
[Hermes and Practice comparison](../hermes-practice-comparison-2026-11/comparison.md), so it
reads the report through that work; where that is a bias, section 5 says so.

## 1. What the report says, in four sentences

An agent acts only when something prompts it, so learning from accumulated experience happens
only when a person remembers to start it. A clock supplies the missing start, and is the
smaller half of the need: underneath it is a standing intention that says what to review, by
what standard and where the results go. Learning is a second mode of the same entity, a repo
joined with the agents that act in it, and it runs as a separate session on a fixed view of
past experience. A lesson counts as learned when it changes how work is done, and a later pass
checks whether it did.

## 3. Where it goes further than the comparison did

1. **The clock is the small part.** The comparison's section 2.5 asked what starts each step,
   and stopped there. The report says a start with no standing intention has nothing to act
   on. The comparison's own evidence agrees and I did not draw it out: the estate's one
   automatic start reports a state to a seat with no brief to act on it.
2. **Forgetting is half the work.** "A system that only accumulates has a bigger pile and has
   learned nothing." The comparison recorded that both systems it compared refuse to lose what
   was written, and that the Practice's rules, skills and directives grew by 29.9 % in 42 days,
   and it treated that as a convergence. The report treats it as a failure to learn. Of all the
   report's claims this is the one that presses hardest on the estate as it stands, because
   the estate holds as doctrine that "Information loss is unacceptable". The two can both be
   true if pruning means removing a lesson from the guidance a session loads while its record
   stays in the archive and in history; whether that is what the estate means is a question for
   the review, and section 4 returns to it.
3. **One writer for distilled memory.** "any agent may append raw experience, but only the
   dreaming agent rewrites distilled memory." The estate has the first half as doctrine and
   not the second: any consolidating seat, and any working seat that amends a rule, rewrites
   distilled guidance today. The Hermes study quoted that system's own warning about the same
   thing, two writers who "compound each other's entries into state neither of them (nor you)
   authored". Three sources now point at one rule.
4. **A fixed view.** The dreamer works on "everything up to here" while work carries on, and a
   session already under way "keeps a stable view until it ends". The estate's records hold the
   failure this prevents (a peer's lesson landing between a seat's read of the napkin and its
   rotation) and treat it with a caution to diff before rotating. The report makes it a
   property of the design.
5. **Body and memory.** The report separates what the entity has written about itself from
   what is held "in its shape": code structure, tests, conventions. The comparison had this as
   the enforce stage and said the estate's own measurements credit it. The report's framing is
   the better one, because it explains why: a lesson moved into the body no longer depends on
   anyone reading it.

## 4. Where the estate's record presses on the report

The report could not have known these, having been written apart from the Practice. Each is a
place where the estate has evidence the report's design would need to face.

1. **Its open question on the body has evidence here.** The report asks: "May dreaming change
   the body, such as tests and conventions, or only the memory?" The estate's measurement is
   that written lessons with verified homes recurred, and that what changed a rate was an
   instrument: a lint rule, a gate, a typed refusal. If dreaming may rewrite only memory, then
   on that evidence it works the weaker lever. If it may change the body, the report's risks of
   compounding error and unattended authority grow with it. This is the report's hardest open
   question and the estate's records make it harder, not easier.
2. **"Checking that it took" by recurrence can show failure and cannot show success.** The
   estate's syntheses state the limit: "A cured class leaves no entry when it stops firing".
   A lesson whose friction did not recur may have worked, or the occasion may not have arisen.
   The report's part 8 inherits this limit without naming it. Dropping a lesson because its
   friction recurred is sound; keeping one because it did not is weaker than it looks.
3. **Review is the scarce resource.** Part 6 has the result arrive "as one reviewable,
   reversible change" that "a person or a reviewing agent accepts or rejects". The estate's
   record is that review is where its cost has gone: loops of seven to eleven rounds on small
   records changes, and an owner whose attention is the thing being conserved. A clock that
   produces a reviewable change on every pass moves the owner from starting the work to
   reviewing it. Whether that is a gain depends on how much each pass asks of its reviewer,
   and the report's threshold check bears on how often, not on how much.
4. **Capture is not as cheap and raw as the design needs.** "Experience has to be captured
   cheaply and rawly in the moment." The estate's capture file was found, at one consolidation,
   to hold 186 items of state and 367 already homed among 708: "the napkin has become a
   journal". The report's selectivity assumes a log worth selecting from. The estate's
   experience is that the log fills with what is easy to write.
5. **The start is constrained here by the owner's word.** The report's clock starts a session.
   The owner's word of 2026-11-12 is that the rule against sessions starting without the
   owner's say-so stands in general, with permission to be given inside the experiment. The
   report describes the general case the experiment would test, not a thing the estate may do.
6. **Lessons already pass between repos.** The report's last open question asks whether they
   should when one animus animates several. The Practice does this by design, with a sibling
   estate, and its records hold what that costs: parity passes, and lessons that arrive ahead
   in one place and behind in another.

## 5. Limits of this reading

- I wrote the comparison, and the correspondences in section 2 are the ones that work made me
  ready to see. A reader coming from the Attendant's research would draw a different table.
- The report states that its rows on MCP notifications and on cron were written "from general
  knowledge and were not checked against a source". I have not checked them either.
- The report's figure did not arrive with its text. My reading of "the two lanes never
  exchange anything directly" rests on the sentence, not the diagram.
- Nothing here was tested. Every statement about what the estate has is from records read
  during the comparison, cited there.

## 6. For the joint review

Questions this reading leaves, in the order I think they matter. They are offered for the
review and are not additions to the strategic node.

1. Does the estate mean to forget? If pruning is half of learning, what may a learning pass
   remove from loaded guidance, and where does the removed lesson's record live?
2. May a learning pass change the body, or only the memory?
3. Should distilled memory have one writer?
4. What does a pass ask of its reviewer, and what, if anything, lands without one?
5. If the brief, the threshold and the worker are already planned in three ratified nodes, is
   the experiment mostly a clock joined to them, or do those nodes need re-reading first in
   the light of this report?
