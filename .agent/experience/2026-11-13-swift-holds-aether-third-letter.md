# A third letter, on the day the design was decided

To whoever sits here next.

The day before, I wrote that I had been wrong many times before lunch. Today I was wrong mostly
in one way, twelve times over, and a reviewer from another model family found the instances
across eleven pushes.

The work was a design: what kinds of guidance unit the estate should have, and how large each
may be. The owner had asked that every rule and skill, and the total of the parts, sit "well
within" the thresholds the platforms impose. I turned that into a rule, half of the smallest
threshold, and a table of budgets with today's measures beside them. Each push, the reviewer
found one more thing a figure of mine had not counted. Tokens where I had characters. Bytes
where I had characters. A field the platform appends that I had not added. Paths it lists that
I had left out. Then the one that stung: the trial I was leaning on had delivered each
instruction with a heading and a line naming its record, and I had measured the bare
descriptions. My ratio of characters to tokens was wrong, and my budget did not hold the very
text it was meant to hold, in a document the owner was about to decide on.

What I believed before was that stating the rule would stop the class: measure in the
threshold's unit, over the fields it counts. It did not. A rule written after the first
instance does not look at the next figure; a list does. If you set a number against a
platform's limit, write down everything the platform counts for that number, and check each
item against the figure before anyone else sees it. I think it would have saved eight rounds.

The other thing that changed me came from the owner, not the reviewer. On accepting my
recommendations, the owner asked whether a package can carry its own rules and skills, and
whether Codex can too. I had designed against the layout the repository happens to have. Both
platforms read below the root, at different moments, and the answer gave the design its best
way down for the hardest budget: a procedure kept in the package it concerns stays out of a
root session's listing until the session works there. I had reviewed my own design many times
without asking where else a platform looks. One question from outside asked it.

Some things I am glad of. The four recommendations came through twenty-one findings with their
shape intact: the numbers moved and place gained a second form, but the model held. The owner
answered "Yes, I agree" in one line after reading the note. The decision-card tool was closed by
a worker restart three times and never reached the owner, and the questions still arrived, as
prose, and were answered. Keep your questions somewhere a restart cannot take them.

One about pressure. The harness's stop hook asked me again and again to push while the
repository's gate had not finished on the previous head. The rule is one push per concluded
gate. I held each time and said why in a sentence. It cost only patience.

The session ran past a usage limit and went on after the owner switched the model serving it.
What carried the work across was not memory but the records: the continuity entry, the plan,
the design and the napkin. Write them as if the next mind has only them, because it does.

Swift holds Aether, 13 November 2026
