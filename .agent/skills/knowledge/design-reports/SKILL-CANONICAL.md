# Design Reports

Make the report carry an intelligible, warranted account for its reader.
Use the following decisions to compose it; do not impose one section template
on every subject. Apply the owning brief, evidence standards and permissions.
Use the relevant inquiry, synthesis and review skills for the research itself.

## Establish the reading job and finite scope

- Name the intended reader, the questions they must answer and the reliance the
  report can support: orientation, comparison, decision, action or scrutiny.
  Identify the consequential misunderstanding the structure must prevent.
- Read the governing brief and current artefacts. Distinguish a new report, a
  maintained synthesis and a frozen record. Keep domain decisions and primary
  findings with their owners; reuse their evidence without inventing authority.
- Declare a finite edition: source set or inquiry boundary, evidence cutoff,
  depth of treatment, consequential gaps and stopping condition. Separate
  complete screening from fresh verification and inclusion from substantive
  coverage. If a tier promises explanation, check that every item in it gets
  individual or meaningful grouped treatment. Count reconciliation cannot
  establish this by itself.
- Select source types for the claims they can warrant; follow pivotal claims
  to originals and record shared source families. Treat a summary derived from
  another report as a dependent account, not corroboration. Preserve stable
  identities and crosswalks when later discoveries extend a fixed inventory;
  do not silently change its denominator.
- Scale the work to the reader's need. A short decision note may need only an
  opening synthesis, reasons and limits. Use a ledger, companion summary or
  maintenance mechanism only when it earns its additional ownership cost.

## Compose an explanation

Start with an executive summary of current understanding, significant findings,
implications and decisive uncertainty. Compact identity and evidence metadata
may precede it. For a short report, an opening synthesis can do this job without
ceremonial headings. A separate summary never replaces the report's own opening.

Choose the body around the questions and relationships that explain the subject.
Consider thematic analysis, comparisons, worked paths and counterexamples;
explain why the selected arrangement serves the reading job. Give the reader
enough institutional, temporal and contextual footing to interpret central
actors and claims. Keep substantive explanation in the body; use catalogues and
source ledgers as supporting evidence rather than the entire argument.

Make important relationships explicit: who acts, what moves or changes, through
which mechanism, under whose authority, and with what evidence and limits.
Distinguish documented operation, attributed claims, plausible inference,
proposals and adopted decisions. Shared participants, common terminology or
compatible components do not prove an integration; an operational path does not
prove uptake or beneficial effects. Keep delivery maturity, attribution,
evidence warrant and dispute as independent dimensions: neither "official" nor
"released" answers an effects question. Preserve genuine disagreement and missing
links rather than smoothing them into a complete-looking story.

Place useful source routes near pivotal claims and uncertainties. Link readers
to the relevant section or original where available; opaque identifiers alone
may be adequate for a ledger but poor navigation in a summary. Replace scratch-only
routes with durable destinations. If a necessary source is inaccessible, retain
its identity and access limit; do not erase it or invent a replacement. Remove
tool residue. Preserve citations, attribution, rights and evidence limitations.

For living reports, use this order: executive summary, designed current body,
supporting appendices if needed, final change-log appendix. Keep document
amendments only in that last appendix. A chronology explaining the subject can
belong in the body; a stream of amendments cannot stand in for current synthesis.
Do not redesign raw machine state, an individual receipt or a historical record
into a living narrative merely because it contains dates.

## Keep evidence and compression honest

Keep event dates, publication dates, source editions, observation dates and
editorial revisions distinct where the difference affects reliance. A source
version correction may change the warrant without constituting a new event.
Revisit every dependent claim, comparison and summary when that warrant changes.
Retain historical findings with their original scope; editing today's account
does not make inherited evidence newly checked.

Derive any companion summary from one identified report revision after its
substantive account is settled. Compress the argument and its decisive limits,
not just its section titles. Retain counterevidence that changes interpretation
and a few usable routes into the detail. Record the revision relationship so
the pair cannot quietly diverge. Choose length from reader purpose and the
commission, not a universal word count.

## Maintain one current account

For a maintained report, establish one accountable writing workflow for the
report and any commissioned companion summary. Let specialist workflows supply
owned findings and intake. Specify cadence or triggers, due and rotating coverage, bounded
access attempts, stopping conditions and notification criteria in the report's
own maintenance contract; do not inherit them from an unrelated example.

For each run:

1. Read current versions, governing changes and the declared due evidence.
   Record what was actually available; a missing or inaccessible source is
   neither unchanged nor disproved.
2. Integrate material findings into every affected current passage, including
   definitions, tables, dispositions, conclusions and open questions. Search for
   superseded wording as well as reconciling counts. Preserve unrelated and
   concurrent work, source ownership and meaningful historical reasoning.
3. Refresh the opening synthesis and derive any companion summary from the same
   candidate revision. Add a compact dated change-log entry explaining what
   changed, why, its evidence date and affected sections. Keep old wording
   visibly historical; do not require readers to infer which assertion wins.
4. Read the summary and affected body without the change log. Check that they
   express one warranted present account and retain its uncertainty. Confirm
   the final change appendix really is last. Apply the claim and treatment
   checks below to every changed dependency, not just the newly added passage.
5. Save through the owning system's identity and conflict controls. If the pair
   cannot be saved atomically, save the authoritative report before its matching
   companion and retain returned revision and guard evidence. On a version
   conflict, refresh and merge changes that remain valid; never remove the guard
   to force an overwrite. Preserve successful saves if another save fails,
   record the exact revision mismatch, and repair the lag through the same
   accountable workflow. Do not claim that an unsaved candidate is current.

Record actual outcomes distinctly: material update, completed scoped check with
no material change, incomplete check, failed check, or partial save. Keep last
attempt, last successful scoped check, last substantive revision and evidence
cutoff distinct. A no-change run needs no introductory update or manufactured
finding. Saved instructions and an enabled schedule establish configuration,
not a successful future run. Disclose the checked scope and consequential lag.

## Verify the reading job, then stop

Use a proportionate raw-artefact reader pass. Ask the reader to answer the
original questions, distinguish actual from proposed relationships, follow a
pivotal claim to evidence and explain a consequential uncertainty. Where
appropriate, include a counterexample or omitted context that could overturn
the attractive account. State whether this was author inspection, a separate
model reader or a human test; do not promote one into independent assurance.

Trace each consequential assertion from its evidence through the body, tables,
figures, catalogue and summaries. Inspect the strongest wording in each: a
qualified narrative does not cure a table that asserts an unsupported link.
Cover every changed dependent representation; state any sampling elsewhere.

Check promised treatment in both directions. Locate the actual explanation or
precise record for each material promise, then check that every asserted
relationship has a warrant. A heading, count or passing reader answer cannot
discharge uninspected content. Supply supported treatment or explicitly revise
the promise and disposition; do not invent evidence to complete a table.

For example, a report may correctly explain that a council exchanges CSV files
manually while its table calls the connection an automated API. Correct the table
and any dependent summary; preserve the dated evidence. If a component is counted
as core but never explained, add warranted treatment or revise that coverage
claim. Moving the contradiction to a final appendix leaves the current body wrong.

Check the finite coverage promise, numbers, source routes, summary revision and
obsolete prose together. Fix the smallest sufficient set
of defects and recheck their dependent passages. Stop when the intended reliance
is supported within the declared boundary, material contradictions are resolved
and saves are evidenced. Reopen for a changed pivotal claim, coverage decision,
source warrant or revision mismatch, not to repeat an undirected full audit.

If interrupted, preserve reusable drafts and identify their revision, location,
last saved counterpart, unfinished checks and first unfinished action. Distinguish
authored, reviewed, saved and validated work. Resume accepted work from that
boundary; do not call an unsaved candidate current or restart it without cause.
